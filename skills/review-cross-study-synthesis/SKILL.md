# Cross-study synthesis

Use the supplied source passages to answer the chapter's research questions.
The chapter outline describes writing objectives and retrieval directions;
it does not prescribe conclusions that the sources must prove. No pre-generated
fact cards or approved claim list is required. Derive each assertion from the
provided passages and preserve their paper identities and assertion ceilings.

For each assertion, provide exact supporting quotes through the requested
structured output. Preserve attribution, negation, study objects, experimental
conditions, numbers, and units. Distinguish reported results, authors'
interpretations, and review-level inferences. Abstract-only sources support
broad attributed framing, not detailed quantitative or mechanistic claims.

For each synthesis unit, prefer this order when the supplied sources permit:

1. State the common pattern or shared scientific result.
2. Compare genuinely commensurable differences across two or more studies.
3. Relate those differences to reported conditions, design, or mechanism evidence.
4. Identify supported exceptions and applicability boundaries.
5. Explain the practical or research implication without extending beyond the sources.

Do not create a cross-study ranking from one paper. Do not directly compare
metrics measured under incompatible definitions or conditions. State evidence
levels when they materially affect interpretation, but do not repeat generic
disclaimers. A missing retrieval result is not proof that a source did not
report something. A negative source statement must itself be supported by the
source text. Omit assertions that the supplied sources cannot support; do not
invent evidence to complete an outline or a comparison quota.

For quantitative comparisons, keep one source-specific result-context record
per experiment with the reported object, conditions, result, and units. Leave
unknown values unspecified. Ordinary narrative synthesis does not require
result-context records. Follow the caller's output schema; do not introduce a
separate planning, fact-extraction, or approval step.

Write for readers of the review. Never mention Matrix, Evidence Package,
indexed evidence, available excerpts, prompts, models, stages, gates, paper IDs,
claim IDs, or evidence keys in manuscript prose.
