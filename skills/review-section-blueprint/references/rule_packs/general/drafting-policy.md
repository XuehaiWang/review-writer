# Scientific safeguards for initial review writing

Use the caller's section-thread policy for organization. This policy defines
scientific accuracy, not a sequence of paragraphs or required data inventory.

- State a conclusion only as strongly as the supplied evidence allows. Separate
  observations, source-author explanations and bounded review interpretations.
- Attribute a finding to the work that established it. Introductory citations
  and reference-list titles do not establish the citing paper's contribution.
- Keep a reported outcome attached to its object, intervention or method,
  population and measurement context. Compare only compatible definitions and
  conditions; otherwise explain the relevant difference without ranking.
- Retain exceptions that change the interpretation. Do not infer failure from
  an unmentioned case or infer a research gap from a missing retrieval result.
- Discuss design, mechanism or causal explanations only when supported. A
  pattern alone is not proof of mechanism or causation.
- Preserve names, units and numerical meaning for details actually selected
  for the argument; do not reconstruct damaged identifiers by guessing.
- Explicit user scope and requested detail remain authoritative. A short
  section may be appropriate when only a bounded finding is supported.
- Sources, not style examples, supply all facts. Do not introduce facts or
  illustrative numbers from a writing rule, another chapter or prior draft.
