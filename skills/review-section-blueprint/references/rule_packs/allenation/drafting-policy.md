# Chemical safeguards for initial review writing

Use the caller's section-thread policy for organization. These are scientific
constraints on selected claims, not an instruction to enumerate every experiment.

- Distinguish the source's own contribution from cited prior work. A reference
  title cannot support experimental results. Use secondary reports with clear
  attribution and do not present them as directly checked primary evidence.
- Keep substrate structure, product class and reaction identity distinct.
  Similar scaffold names do not prove identical structures or scope.
- Preserve the role of the metal, ligand, additive and reaction partner when
  that role matters to the claim. Do not interchange catalytic and stoichiometric
  use, direct and telescoped procedures, or substrate and product identities.
- Attach every reported yield or selectivity to its particular experiment and
  product. Separate axial, central and other stereochemical outcomes; ee, dr,
  regioselectivity, conversion and recovered-material measurements are not
  interchangeable. Do not rank methods from incompatible scope maxima.
- Distinguish a proposed catalytic cycle from observed intermediates or control
  experiments. A control that disfavors one route does not prove the entire cycle.
- Relate scope and failures to structural or experimental differences only where
  the source supports that relation. Preserve counterexamples that change a
  conclusion; absence from a scope table does not demonstrate failure.
- Preserve chemical names, formulas, units and stereochemical meaning when used.
  Consult intact supplied occurrences for damaged formulas; otherwise use a
  supported broader term or omit the nonessential detail, never guess subscripts.
- Claims of generality, practicality or superiority require appropriate evidence.
  One scale experiment or derivatization supports its specific demonstrated use,
  not industrial feasibility or universal stereochemical retention.
- All facts must come from the current sources, not historical style examples.
  Explicit user scope and detailed requests take precedence over brevity.
